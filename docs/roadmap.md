# Roadmap

Implements D14 (`docs/analysis/architecture-discussion/05-resolution.md`), updated by the owner's answers of 2026-10-10 (`docs/open-questions.md`; decisions 016–027). Phase names and gates are shared with `dag.md`, whose nodes map to the rulebook task ids (`task-index.md`). Durations are **Hypotheses** (owner Q14: shown as labelled Hypotheses and revised at each gate); every gate is defined by evidence, not by date.

## Pilot order (owner Q7)

Wand v1 is the Phase 1 smoke fixture; Belt v5 and Tunic v10 complete the first production-parity pilot sequence in Phase 2, with Tunic v10 as the milestone that counts. Once Tunic v10 passes its parity gate (G2), the Assets3D "painting is always in Substance" rule is amended for the proven scope (owner Q13; `P3-CUTOVER`). Locked bracers and the historical hat v5 are early procedural/native-bake fixtures in Phase 3; hat v8 and body v6b give current channel/export and multi-set coverage in Phase 4. None of the first three covers native bake, proprietary materials, 3D generators or multi-set behaviour; milestones are named by what they prove and never called "full replacement" while required gaps are open (owner Q5).

## Rules

1. Feasibility work (procedurals, Iray reference capture, native bake) starts in Phase 0 and may overlap Phases 1–2; a pilot success never closes a feasibility gap.
2. Each gate joins every mandatory item of its phase and requires the previous gate (`dag.md` gate nodes); a single work package cannot close a gate, and G5 cannot be reached without every mandatory node.
3. Every phase ends with its gate evidence in `parity/reports/` (numbers and hashes only; oracle blobs stay in the private store), a `.rulebook` archive of its tasks and a regenerated capability manifest.
4. Tunic recovery is an early binding milestone; if it blocks on a schema or binding problem, that investigation moves earlier without dropping the smoke fixtures.
5. Software certification never grants artistic, alpha or rig approval of existing assets; those stay in the Assets3D manifests.
6. Durations are labelled Hypotheses and are revised at each gate (owner Q14). The Wand v1 pilot (P1-WAND) is the first schedule checkpoint: when it completes, pass or fail, this roadmap records a re-forecast of every remaining phase from the pilot's measured wall-clock, failure causes and exception counts, marking which estimates are measured and which remain extrapolated (decision 035).
7. Brush strokes are out of scope (Q5) and migration uses only Painter's documented API (Q6). If a required fixture cannot be migrated through documented APIs, record `not_migratable` with the reason and keep its affected gate blocked. Excluding or replacing that fixture requires an explicit owner scope decision recorded in the capability manifest and gate report; reporting the limitation alone never closes the gate. Existing paint content may be imported as bitmaps when the documented export route preserves the required output (`dag.md`, corpus migration assumption).

8. Before input-dependent Belt or Tunic rebuild/recording work, complete one bounded inventory of identified sources using the searches already documented in the pilot tasks. Publish present/missing inputs and identity evidence, preserve available or recovered bytes and the request/envelope/reference by verified hash in the private store, and do not repeat a sweep without a newly identified source. Restoring verified originals does not require a new scope decision. A recreated, replaced or excluded reference requires an explicit recorded owner scope decision; reuse any pending question and retain the existing reference. The inventory checkpoint produces one decision package when needed, never a silently changed golden or a gate pass. Independent work continues while that choice is unresolved.
9. Wand remains the next delivery checkpoint. Before each unresolved parity experiment batch, record its hypothesis, expected discriminating evidence and finite wall-clock budget in the owning task's evidence record. At budget expiry or the declared stopping condition, stop that batch and publish elapsed time, remaining residuals and the next decision: continue with a new discriminating hypothesis, propose an evidenced owner exception, or leave the capability blocked. No budget expiry grants a tolerance, exception or scope change. A new batch needs a recorded hypothesis and budget; rerunning the same investigation without new evidence does not reset the checkpoint. At a blocked Wand checkpoint, record an interim schedule update from measured effort and unresolved dependencies; on the pilot's pass/fail completion, perform the full remaining-phase re-forecast required by rule 6, distinguishing measured estimates from hypotheses.

## Schedule checkpoint: Wand v1 (2026-11-03, decision 035)

**Measured from the slice.**
- **Wall-clock.** The task was created on 2026-10-10 and its R9 goldens were admitted on 2026-10-12 (decision 180). The first full Tier 1 runs came on 2026-10-13 (decisions 223, 245), and the pass under EXC-010 on 2026-11-03: 24 calendar days. One Tier 1 run takes 232 s.
