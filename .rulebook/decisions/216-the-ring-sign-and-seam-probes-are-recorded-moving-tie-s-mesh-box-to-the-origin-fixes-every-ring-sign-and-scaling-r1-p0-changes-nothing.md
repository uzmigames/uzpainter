# 216. The ring-sign and seam probes are recorded: moving tie's mesh box to the origin fixes every ring sign, and scaling r1-p0 changes nothing

**Status**: accepted
**Date**: 2026-10-13
**Related Tasks**: phase1.25_pilot-wand, phase1.18_export-engine

## Context

Decision 210 §5 asked for two cheap recordings to test its lead, that the `infinite` ring sign follows float32 rounding of positions normalised by the mesh's world bounding box:
- **P-tie-dyadic-box.** tie's three UV islands, with the world quads moved so that the mesh's box is centred at the origin with half-extent 16. The reading predicts the fixed sign (+u, +v) on every ring texel.
- **P-ramp-17.** `seam-ramp-r1-p0` with its world positions scaled so that the box's largest extent is 34. Decision 210 predicted a mixed sign on r1.

Decision 211 §5 named `export-padding-seam-field-1024` as a second equation per seam-ring texel, once its field is decoded. Its seam mesh has r15-p0's island B, and island A 64 texels further left, on the tile's left border. A position-encoded twin gives that equation without decoding anything, at the cost of one more probe.

The model panel was not consulted. Decisions 210 and 211 fix the contents, and the rest follows the P-ramp and P-tie pattern.

## Decision

1. **Three requests**, generated by `recording_requests.padding_sign_probes()`, each the decision-129 probe sequence (User0 RGB32F, one fill with the float UV ramp, `infinite` and `passthrough` at OpenEXR 32f, each export step done twice):

   | Golden | Mesh | Ramp | Resolution |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `export-padding-tie-512-box16` | `tie-box16.obj`: tie's UVs and faces, quads at x ∈ [−16, −6], [−5, 5], [6, 16] cm, z ∈ [−5, 5] | `uv-ramp-512` | 512 |
   | `export-padding-ramp-r1-p0-box17` | `seam-ramp-r1-p0-box17.obj`: r1-p0's UVs and faces, positions × 1.7 (x 0..34, z 0..17 cm) | `uv-ramp-n1024` | 1024 |
   | `export-padding-seam-ramp-1024` | `seam.obj` (as `export-padding-seam-field-1024`) | `uv-ramp-n1024` | 1024 |

   - `request_fixtures.tie` takes the quad centres, and `ramp_seam` the box extent. With their defaults, `tie.obj` and the nine P-ramp meshes keep their bytes and decision 129's SHA-256s.
   - No imported image stem ends in a number from 1001 up (decision 166).
2. **Recorded** on 2026-10-13 in Painter `12.1.5+js-api.1.1.20`:

## Findings

These counts come from one quick pass over the r1 `infinite` export, run from the private store. They use decision 206's straight-edge set: uncovered texels with exactly three covered neighbours in a line. The fixed rule is +x on horizontal edges and −row on vertical edges.

| Probe | Horizontal ring: +x / −x | Vertical ring: −row / +row | Fixed rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `export-padding-tie-512` (decision 160) | 236 / 136 | 194 / 178 | 430 of 744 |
| `export-padding-tie-512-box16` | **372 / 0** | **372 / 0** | **744 of 744** |

1. **P-tie-dyadic-box confirms the bounding-box reading.** With the same UVs and the same covered texels, moving the quads so that the mesh box is centred at the origin with half-extent 16 turns tie-512's mixed sign into the fixed sign on all 744 ring texels.
   - The ring sign depends on the world positions, through the box, and not on UV space.
   - This is the outcome decision 210 predicted.
2. **P-ramp-17's `infinite` export is byte-identical to `export-padding-ramp-r1-p0-v2`'s** (`434cddfc…`). The sign stays fixed, against decision 210's stated prediction. That prediction did not follow from its own candidate rule, though:
   - p/h − c/h normalises the scaled vertices (0, 17, 34; 0, 17), with c = (17, 0, 8.5) and h = 17, to exactly −1, 0, 1 and −0.5, 0.5;
   - the reciprocal form gives the same values, because 17·fl(1/17), 34·fl(1/17) and 8.5·fl(1/17) round to 1, 2 and 0.5;
   - that is the same normalised mesh as r1-p0's.

   So the probe does not separate the candidate from the fixed rule. What it does show:
   - Painter's padding is invariant under a uniform world scale of 1.7;
   - no computation on un-normalised world positions decides the sign.
3. **The seam probe gives the second equation decision 211 asked for.** On `export-padding-seam-ramp-1024`:
   - B's three non-seam ring edges are bit-identical to `export-padding-ramp-r15-p0-v2`'s: 770 of the 1,028 ring texels around B, one right-column texel apart;
   - B's seam ring row differs on all 256 texels, because its far-island taps now read A's positions 0.0625 lower;
   - on vertical edges every straight-edge ring texel takes the fixed sign (890 of 890), whereas r15-p0-v2 has 138 of 1,272 against it;
   - A touches the tile's left border here, so its left ring column does not exist.

## Consequences

- Decision 210 4(a) narrows: the sign is decided in mesh-box-normalised object space, and is fixed when the normalised vertices are dyadic. The comparison made there is still not identified, and item 2.7 stays unchecked. `infinite` padding on islanded sets stays refused (`padding.h`, `NotYetSupported` naming R-090). No production code, semantics entry, capability or conformance case changes.
- The next offline pass has three new records:
  - the tie pair, which differs only in world x;
  - a scale-invariance control;
  - the seam pair (r15-p0 against seam-ramp), for decision 211's weights (4(a)) and tap choice (4(b)).
- A probe that would separate the candidate rule needs a box whose normalised vertices are not dyadic while the UVs stay dyadic. An example is tie with its centre moved off the origin by a non-dyadic amount, as on the original tie. That needs no new recording: the original tie is that case.
- No Painter recording is queued after this one.
