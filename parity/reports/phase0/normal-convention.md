## 8. External consumer: UzEngine (task 2.6)

UzEngine's own executables, built from its repository as an external consumer (no UzEngine code is linked into UzPainter): commit `f4dcfc467b79b0538ef5f0aa615f16e15e8820ab`, extracted with `git archive`, provisioned with its `scripts/setup-native.ps1` and built with `zig build -Doptimize=ReleaseFast` (Zig 0.15.2, VS 2022 Build Tools 17.14, Windows SDK 10.0.26100.0) outside both repositories. The plane-dome low mesh was cooked by `uzmesh` from the OBJ and shaded by `uzengine --hidden --screenshot` with the prototype's OpenGL map of the plane-dome pair (`PerFragment-inverse-opengl`): grey, rough, non-metal material, normalIntensity 1, one directional light; anti-aliasing, post effects, GI, AO, sky and auto exposure switched off by cvar; camera looking along the plane normal.

**Verdict: UzEngine reads green as -v (DirectX convention) for meshes `uzmesh` cooks from OBJ.** With the light from the OBJ -Z side (the +v side of the UVs), the half of the dome whose texels lean towards +v in the OpenGL map is the darker one, and the reverse with the light from +Z; the red control behaves as expected (light from +X brightens the +X half). The G-buffer world normal agrees: in the green > 1/2 half its mean points towards OBJ +Z (-v). This matches the engine's source at the same commit: `uzmesh` flips V on OBJ import and builds the bitangent along dP/dV of the flipped V (Lengyel tangents), and its G-buffer shader decodes `rg * 2 - 1` with no green flip. An OpenGL-convention map (Painter's or UzPainter's) therefore needs its green inverted, or a DirectX export, for this consumer path.

Side observations: `uzmesh`'s OBJ path ignores the file's `vn` and recomputes normals from the reversed winding, so the cooked plane's shading normal points down and the renders light the plane from below (elevation -35 degrees); the engine loads the 16-bit PNG but decodes every texture to 8 bits (the 16-bit and 8-bit maps rendered identically).
