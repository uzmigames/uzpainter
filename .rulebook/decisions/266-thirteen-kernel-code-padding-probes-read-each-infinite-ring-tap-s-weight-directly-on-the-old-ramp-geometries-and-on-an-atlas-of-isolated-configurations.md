# 266. Thirteen kernel-code padding probes read each `infinite`-ring tap's weight directly, on the old ramp geometries and on an atlas of isolated configurations

**Status**: accepted
**Date**: 2026-10-18
**Related Tasks**: phase1.25_pilot-wand, phase1.18_export-engine, phase1.16_export-processing-encoders

## Context

On 2026-10-18 the owner ruled that the Wand must match Painter everywhere, padding included. Exact `infinite` padding on UV islands (item 2.7 of `phase1.25_pilot-wand`) is the critical path, and offline fits have stalled (decisions 176, 184, 197, 206, 210, 211, 216, 217, 220, 261, 262, 263, 265).

**What is known:**
- the gutter is a jump flood seeded by the ring (exact, decision 197);
- away from seams, the ring is a weighted mean of the covered 8-neighbours: side weight 1, diagonal f/(1 − f) with f = 0.4142245, and on a straight edge a shift of about 24.9·2⁻¹⁵ texel along the edge, read as one diagonal weakened by 1/(1 + e) (decisions 206, 217), its sign the nearer diagonal in mesh-box-normalised object space (decision 261);
- across a seam, the ring is a mean of at most 4 taps (own 8-neighbours plus a strip of the far island), with weights close to 1/d but not radial (decisions 211, 262, 265).

**Still open:** the seam weights, r15-p25 B's 127 texels, 18 of 40 seam corners, the sphere poles, the folded seams of the bevelled cube, and where the straight-edge factor comes from.

**Why the fits stall.** Every padding probe so far carries the UV ramp, R = (x + 0.5)/N and G = (y + 0.5)/N. A weighted mean of a linear ramp gives only the kernel's **centroid**: two numbers per ring texel. Any kernel with the same centroid gives the same value. So every fit had to assume the tap set and the kernel shape, then fit the weights through that assumption. "1/(1 + e) on one diagonal", "f slightly above √2 − 1" and "4 taps with non-radial weights" are all centroid readings. None was measured tap by tap.

This decision designs a small set of new recordings that read each tap's weight directly. No Painter run is part of it.

## Decision

### 1. The kernel code: eight RGB32F channels that read each tap

Each probe adds User0–User7 as RGB32F. It puts one authored float32 bitmap at the document size in each channel, then exports all eight maps at OpenEXR 32f with `infinite` and with `passthrough`. The generator is `request_fixtures.kernel_onehot` / `kernel_code`.

| Channel | Content | What it gives per ring texel |
|---|---|---|
| User0–User2 | **One-hot classes**: texel (x, y), with x the column and y the row from the top, has class k = x mod 3 + 3·(y mod 3), and slot k of the 9 scalars is 1.0, the others 0 | The 9 texels of any 3×3 window own one scalar each. A ring value in slot k **is** that tap's normalised weight wₖ/Σw, read with no model |
| User3 | The UV ramp (`uv-ramp-512`, `uv-ramp-n1024`, `uv-ramp-n2048`, new `uv-ramp-n256`) | The centroid, as before. On the re-recorded geometries it must reproduce the old records bit for bit, which checks that a re-recording is deterministic |
| User4, User5 | Codes d and e: pseudo-random values k/256 (k in 1..255), each tiled with a period of 17 columns by 13 rows | 6 more scalars. They locate taps beyond the 3×3 window: the seam's far strip, poles, any wider kernel |
| User6 | f = (d + e)/2 | **Superposition control.** Painter's f must equal (F(d) + F(e))/2, up to the output rounding |
| User7 | g = 1 − d | **Affine control.** It must equal 1 − F(d), which shows the weights are normalised |

Every value is exact in float32.

**Decoding.** For each ring texel:
- the candidates are the covered texels within 3 texels in UV, plus the far island's texels near the unfolded point;
- the 17 independent scalars (User0–User5) are solved by non-negative least squares over those candidates;
- a decode is accepted only if it reproduces all 17 scalars on Painter's 2⁻²⁴ output grid (decision 206) and passes both controls.

Inside any 3×3 window the one-hot slots alone are exact. Two candidates share a code vector only at offsets that are multiples of 51 columns and 39 rows, far outside any candidate set, and the ramp also gives each tap's absolute position.

**Fixtures.** The codes are periodic so that the 28 kernel bitmaps and `uv-ramp-n256` stay at 3.5 MB together. At 2048, each one-hot file is about 85 KB and each code file about 480 KB.

### 2. Thirteen requests

All are generated by `recording_requests.padding_kernel_probes()`. Each runs decision 129's sequence (`kernel_probe`): one fill, Normal at opacity 1, default projection read back, every export step done twice.

### 3. The kernel atlas

One texture set, `uzp_kernel_atlas`, made of separate 3D pieces. The generator is `request_fixtures.kernel_atlas`, with meshes `kernel-atlas-n{512,1024,2048}.obj` and `kernel-atlas-n1024-moved.obj`.

**Layout.** It is given in texels of a 512 px tile, inside texels 16..496, with every item at least 8 texels from every other (measured minimum 9). At resolution N, the UVs are u = column/N and v = 1 − row/N, so every resolution sees the same texel configuration.

**Geometry.**
- Flat islands are isometric: 1 texel = 1 cm.
- Every island corner sits on a quarter or eighth texel, so no texel centre lies on an edge. A test checks this.
- Two anchor triangles that cover no texel centre fix the box at exactly [−256, 256] cm in x and z.
- Pieces of one item share their 3D vertices (one `v` line each), so their seams are real seams.
- Every face carries its own normal.

| Group | Items, with covered texels | Open question |
|---|---|---|
| Lone taps | `point` (1) | A ring texel with one side tap or one diagonal tap: is it a copy, and does any shift exist without a second tap? |
| Pairs | `pair-axis-x/y`; `pair-diagonal` and `pair-antidiagonal` as one thin quad; `pair-diagonal-two-pieces`; `pair-knight-two-pieces` (2 each) | Pairwise weights: side against diagonal, the two-tap f directly. One quad against two pieces shows whether 3D connectivity changes tap choice or weight |
| Gaps | `gap-x`, `gap-y` and `gap-diagonal-two-pieces` (2 each, 2 texels apart) | Two equidistant taps from different islands: the tie weight, and whether e appears without a straight edge |
| Corners | `l-tromino` (3, one triangle, one concave corner); `block-2/3/4` (4, 9, 16); `diamond-4` (41, 45° staircase edges) | Convex and concave corners, and the staircase ring. The weights at decision 206's f |
| Edges | `row-` and `column-3/8/32/128`, `row-384`, `block-32` (1,024) | The straight-edge factor against edge length and island thickness. r15's 384-texel island is the one size where the sign fails (decision 261) |
| UV seams | Two 3D squares sharing one edge, B its own UV island: `seam-r1-single` (1 texel a side), `seam-r1-turn90` (2×2 each), `seam-r1.5` (3×3 and 2×2), `seam-r1.5-single` (1 a side), `seam-r2-turn90` (4×4 and 2×2), `seam-r2-single-b` (2×2 and 1), `seam-r1.25` (5×5 and 4×4), `seam-r1-turn30` (32, non-dyadic unfolded distances) | Cross-seam tap choice and weights with one to four far candidates, so decision 262's 4-tap ambiguity cannot arise. Ratios 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2; turns 0, 30 and 90 degrees; both seam ends of each pair are seam corners |
| Folded seams | `seam-r1-turn90-fold90`, `seam-r2-turn90-fold90` (the flat twins folded 90° down), `seam-r1-turn90-fold53` (5×5 each, folded atan(4/3), corners at whole centimetres) | Unfolded against 3D distance: a flat twin and a folded twin with the same UVs |
| Cube corners | `corner-r1` (three 4×4 faces, ratio 1, turned 0/90/180); `corner-r1-r1.5-r2` (6×6, 4×4 and 3×3) | Three seams ending at one 3D vertex, with seam corners where more than two islands meet |
| Poles | `pole-4`, `pole-8`, `pole-12` (4, 8 and 12 triangles of a flat square fan meeting at its centre); `pole-8-pyramid` (the apex lifted 8 cm). In UV, the triangles stand side by side, each with its own copy of the apex, as at a UV sphere's pole (144, 256, 120, 256 texels) | The pole rule in isolation, against valence and flat versus curved |

### 4. Void rules and recording

- **Void rules, as in decision 129, now per channel:**
  - a `passthrough` texel that differs from its source on a covered texel voids the record;
  - so does a template capture showing a User channel other than RGB32F;
  - a User3 export on #5–#13 that differs from the old ramp record on a covered texel shows the probe did not load as designed. Report it.
- **The session.** One Legacy session under the Assets3D lock (`parity/RECORDER.md` §11.4), the two 2048 px probes last.
- **Estimated Painter time: about 10 to 20 minutes in all.**
  - Decision 216's three one-channel probes took 14 s of recording.
  - These carry 8 channels and 32 map exports each, so roughly 20 to 60 s at 256 to 1024 px and 2 to 3 minutes at 2048 px.
  - The private store grows by roughly 2 to 3 GB of OpenEXR 32f.

### 5. The model panel

The model panel (GPT-6-astra, Kimi-k3, Grok-4.5) reviewed a first draft of 11 recordings. It had no one-hot controls, no phase-0 seam probes, no cube corners, one pole valence and no turned seam.

**Adopted:**
- the superposition and affine controls (GPT, Kimi), as User6 and User7 in every recording rather than as extra recordings;
- r2-p0 and r15-p0, to separate phase from scale (Kimi);
- the cube corners, as seam termini (Kimi);
- the 53° fold and the pole valences 4 and 12 (Kimi);
- the 30°-turned seam, for non-dyadic unfolded distances (Grok).

**Not adopted:**
- non-periodic codes (GPT). They would cost tens of MB of fixtures (about 12 MB for each 2048 px code bitmap alone), and the periodic tiles collide only far outside any candidate set;
- a second moved box (Kimi). One moved box already tests decision 261's rule on held-out geometry;
- separate zero-bitmap recordings (GPT). The affine control covers additive bias inside each recording.

## Consequences

- **Nothing in production changes.** `src/export/padding.h` keeps decision 223's rule; item 2.7 stays unchecked and EXC-007 unchanged.
- **The repository gains:**
  - 13 requests under `parity/envelopes/painter-12.1.5_js-api.1.1.20/requests/tier0/semantics/`;
  - 29 bitmaps and 4 meshes under `parity/fixtures/tier0-semantics/`;
  - two tests in `tests/parity/test_recording_requests.py`: the one-hot classes and code controls, and the atlas coverage, spacing, resolution scaling and box;
  - a §11.4 paragraph in `parity/RECORDER.md`.
- **After the session,** the offline pass reads every ring texel's taps and weights directly instead of fitting them through a centroid. Decision 265's open items (a)–(d) and the folded-seam rule each get a probe whose texels carry one unknown each.
- **Not requested:**
  - tile-wrap rings (decision 265: 96 of 96 exact);
  - the gutter (decision 197: exact);
  - the r1 phases +0.25 and +0.75, and r2-p75 and r15-p75, which repeat the phase classes of #8–#11.
